# Hiring without whiteboards: work from anywhere

Filtered from [poteto/hiring-without-whiteboards](https://github.com/poteto/hiring-without-whiteboards/blob/main/README.md) on 3 November 2026.

The source list has 761 companies. 249 mention remote work. 73 list the location as only `Remote`, with no office city. That word does not mean any country. This file keeps a company only when its own careers page says people can work from anywhere, with no location restriction.

You still need the legal right to work where you live. A company that says "anywhere" can still refuse sanctioned countries, and a single job post can add a timezone or country rule. Read the posting before you apply.

Interview notes below are copied from the README. Location policy is from each company's site, checked the same day.

## Companies

### Automattic

- Careers: https://automattic.com/work-with-us/
- Policy: "Work from anywhere with internet access, on your own schedule." About 1,380 people in 82 countries. Pay is based on skills, not location.
- Catch: expect to travel about three to four weeks a year for team meetups. Some benefits vary by location.
- Interview (from the README): short take-home code test, then a part-time, paid project.

### Buffer

- Careers: https://buffer.com/journey
- Policy: "add teammates from anywhere in the world" and work wherever you are happiest. Team spread across 22 countries.
- Catch: some roles are limited to a timezone, and the job advert says so.
- Interview (from the README): video interviews, a code walkthrough of real code, then a 45-day full-time paid contract on production code.

### Colonist

- Careers: https://colonist.io/careers
- Policy: fully remote and asynchronous. "Work from anywhere and choose your own hours."
- Interview (from the README): a call, then a real product coding assignment. Their careers page also describes a short paid-style take-home.

### Doist

- Careers: https://www.todoist.com/careers
- Policy: "Todoisters can work from anywhere in the world. We never place restrictions on locations." This is the clearest statement in the set. Pay is adjusted for location.
- Interview (from the README): personal interviews with part of the team and a take-home programming assignment. The careers page now also describes a Google Meet interview, a task, reference checks, and a background check.

### Hashnode

- Careers: https://careers.hashnode.com/jobs
- Policy: "Work From Anywhere" is listed as a company perk, next to asynchronous work.
- Catch: the job board also has city filters (Bengaluru, Dover) and they mention H-1B sponsorship, so confirm the specific role is not tied to a city.
- Interview (from the README): intro call, take-home coding exercise based on a real example, discussion of that task, then a culture-fit session.

### Remote

- Careers: https://remote.com/careers
- Openings: https://remote.com/openings
- Policy: no office. "Most of our jobs are globally remote." They tell you to read the Practicals section of each advert for location or timezone limits.
- Catch: payroll and country-specialist roles are often tied to one country. Skip those.
- Interview (from the README): recruiter call if needed, manager call, code exercise, team call.

### X-Team

- Careers: https://x-team.com/careers
- Policy: "Fully remote. Paid in USD." "Earn in USD, wherever you are" and "from wherever you call home." Developers in 50+ countries. You are matched to a client team in your own timezone, so the timezone is yours, not a required region.
- Catch: this is a contractor community, not a single-employer staff job. They do not publish a sentence as flat as Doist's "we never place restrictions on locations."
- Interview (from the README): a short Node.js challenge, culture questions, a creative mock project, then a discussion of tradeoffs. Top candidates may get 10–30 days of paid training.
